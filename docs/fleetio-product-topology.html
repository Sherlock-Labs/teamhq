Where it breaks: Ring 2

All five Ring 2 modules are behind one Premium tier. A fleet needing only Parts subsidizes Tires, Billing, and Warranty. Shop Network is free in Professional — no upgrade path. In-house shop fleets and outsource-everything fleets have near-zero Ring 2 overlap but pay the same price. Only large private fleets with full in-house shops use all 5 modules.

The Recommendation

Tiered core, modular depth, platform add-ons

Rings 0–1: Tiered. Everyone follows the same tracking → operations progression. Current Essential/Professional boundary is correct.

Ring 2: Modular. Parts & Inventory, Shop Network, Tire Management, Billing, and Intelligence as individually purchasable modules (or pre-built bundles like "In-House Shop" or "Outsourced").

Ring 3: Platform add-ons. API, AI, premium analytics — available to any tier, different buyer (IT, executives).

Seam Analysis

Strong Seams — clean cuts

  • Essential → Professional (Ring 0 → 1)Work orders are the dividing line. Already well-placed.
  • Parts & InventoryOwn objects, users, procurement workflow. Zero coupling with Shop Network.
  • Shop NetworkParallel path to in-house maintenance. Different users entirely.
  • Tire ManagementSelf-contained data model. Only trucking/transport needs it.
  • BillingDifferent business model. Different buyer (shop manager).
  • AnalyticsWas already a separate paid add-on. Proven.
  • APIDifferent buyer (IT). Could be add-on for any tier.

Weak Seams — don't cut here

  • Work Orders ↔ Reminders ↔ Service ProgramsOne continuous workflow. Can't sell WOs without reminders.
  • Inspections ↔ Issues ↔ Work OrdersSingle pipeline. Cutting between Issues and WOs breaks discovery → resolution.
  • Telematics ↔ Fuel GPS AlertsFraud detection requires GPS data. Can't split alert from source.
  • Warranty ↔ Work OrdersWarranty alert surfaces inside the WO. Without WOs it's a static field.
  • Automations ↔ EverythingHorizontal layer. Can't scope to specific modules without bizarre limits.
Automations and Telematics are platform capabilities, not modules.

They cross every boundary. Keep them in Ring 1 — they work across whatever Ring 2 modules you purchase.

Ring 2 modules have bimodal willingness-to-pay

Tire Management value is either very high (trucking: tires = $8-12K/yr/vehicle) or zero (pest control: standard tires, outsource everything). When WTP is bimodal, bundling in a tier over-charges some and under-charges others. This is the textbook signal for modular pricing.

Adjacent Capabilities (What's Missing)

Draw packaging boundaries around the future shape, not just what exists today.

Motor Pool / Vehicle Reservations

Shared vehicle check-out/check-in, reservation scheduling, pool utilization tracking. Critical for PubSec — cities, universities, and government agencies manage shared pools, not assigned vehicles. A verticalized Ring 2 module that doesn't exist today. Example of how "depth" can mean industry-specific workflow, not just deeper maintenance features.

Route Planning & Dispatch

Know where vehicles are (Telematics) → optimize where they go. Natural Ring 1 extension.

ELD / HOS / IFTA

Delegated to partners (Motive, Samsara). Major gap for trucking fleets.

Benchmarking

8M vehicles, 14M repair orders → "your cost per mile is 23% above peers." Data product waiting to happen.

Embedded Payments

14M repair orders with no payment facilitation. Natural Shop Network + Billing extension.

Extended Warranty Processing

Auto Integrate acquisition (2025). Moving from tracking to processing warranty claims.

AI-Powered Insights

Smart Uploads proves AI works. 2026 strategy: predictive maintenance, utilization optimization.

Parts should absorb Procurement. Shop Network should absorb Payments. Analytics should absorb Benchmarking.

The module boundaries you draw now need room for these without another packaging overhaul.
